# First Chimney: Operations Playbook

How a one-person chimney company runs like a ten-person company. Written for Andrew, with Dan as the systems partner. Use what fits; change anything that doesn't.

No prices or wages are filled in here on purpose. Those are Andrew's numbers. The worksheets show how to set them.

---

## 1. The weekly scorecard (CEO, 10 minutes every Monday)

Track these in the GHL dashboard (see the automation playbook §4) or on paper. If a number drops two weeks in a row, that is where to spend time.

| Number | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| New leads | Is marketing working? |
| Minutes to first call back | Speed wins jobs. Aim for under 15 minutes in daytime. |
| Appointments booked | Leads turning into visits |
| Close rate on estimates | Sales skill and pricing |
| Average ticket | Upsell and repair mix |
| Revenue collected | Cash, not promises |
| Reviews received | Future leads |
| Customers on the yearly reminder list | Next year's revenue, already earned |

## 2. Pricing worksheet (CFO)

Price every service from real costs, not from what the cheapest competitor charges.

**Step 1: True hourly cost of the business**
```
Monthly fixed costs (truck payment, fuel avg, insurance, phone, software, tools, marketing)  = $_____
Owner pay target per month                                                                  = $_____
Billable hours per month (be honest: driving and quoting are not billable)                   = _____ hrs
Cost per billable hour = (fixed costs + owner pay) / billable hours                          = $_____ /hr
```

**Step 2: Price a job**
```
Hours on site + drive time            _____ hrs x cost per hour  = $_____
Materials (cap, crown mix, liner kit)                            = $_____
Disposal / equipment rental                                      = $_____
Subtotal                                                         = $_____
Profit margin (pick a target, e.g. 20-30%)                       = $_____
PRICE                                                            = $_____
```

**Step 3: Sanity checks**
- If a price doesn't cover cost per hour, it isn't a price, it's a donation.
- The $199 holiday special is a *front door*, not a profit center. It works because inspections find real repair work and every customer goes on the yearly reminder list. Track how many $199 jobs turn into repairs.
- Take a deposit on masonry and liner jobs (materials up front). Put it in writing on the estimate.
- Set aside a fixed percentage of every payment for taxes in a separate account, the day it comes in. Ask an accountant for the right percentage.

## 3. Phone script (sales)

Answer: *"First Chimney, this is Andrew."*

1. **Listen first.** "What's going on with the chimney?"
2. **Qualify in 4 questions:**
   - What do you burn, and how often?
   - When was it last cleaned or inspected?
   - Anything you've noticed: smoke, smell, water, debris?
   - Where's the house? (confirm it's in the service area)
3. **Recommend one clear next step:** "The best place to start is a cleaning and inspection. That way I can tell you exactly what's going on, with photos."
4. **Book it on the call.** "I have Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon. Which works better?" (Two options, not "when are you free?")
5. **Set expectations:** "I'll text you a confirmation. Please don't burn for 24 hours before so it's cool."

If they ask for a price on a repair over the phone: *"I'd be guessing without seeing it, and I don't want to quote you wrong. The inspection tells us exactly what it needs."*

## 4. On-site visit (operations)

**Before you knock**
- [ ] Truck parked so it doesn't block the driveway
- [ ] Drop cloths, vacuum, camera, ladder, PPE ready
- [ ] Customer's notes from GHL read (health check answers are in the note)

**At the door:** introduce yourself by name, confirm what they asked for, ask where pets and kids are.

**During the work**
- [ ] Drop cloths down before any tool comes out
- [ ] Photos of: firebox, damper, smoke chamber, flue (camera), crown, cap, flashing, brick and mortar
- [ ] Photos of anything wrong, with something for scale

**Walkthrough (the most important 10 minutes)**
1. Show the photos on your phone. Good things first.
2. Explain each problem in one sentence: what it is, why it matters, what happens if it waits.
3. Sort into three groups: **safety now**, **should do this year**, **can wait / monitor**.
4. Give the written estimate on the spot when possible (GHL estimates or invoices from your phone). Options help: a repair-only option and a full-fix option.
5. Never pressure. "Take your time. The photos and estimate will be in your email."

**Before you leave**
- [ ] Area cleaner than you found it
- [ ] Photos uploaded to the opportunity in the GHL app, findings typed in
- [ ] Opportunity moved to `Job Complete` or `Estimate Sent` (this fires the review request or the estimate follow-up automatically)

## 5. Hiring the first helper (recruitment)

The careers page (`/careers`) is live and sends applicants to GHL tagged `Job Applicant`. Post the link (`firstchimneycontracting.com/jobs`) on Facebook, Indeed, Nextdoor and local trade-school job boards.

**Phone screen (10 minutes)**
1. What kind of work have you done with your hands?
2. How do you feel about heights and ladders?
3. Do you have a valid driver's license and reliable transportation?
4. Tell me about a time you had to be careful in someone else's home.
5. When could you start, and what days can you work?

**Working interview:** a paid half day on a real job. You learn more in four hours than in any interview. Watch for: on time, asks questions, careful with the customer's home, doesn't stand around.

**Before the first day:** confirm workers' comp and liability coverage include employees, and get the new-hire paperwork from an accountant or payroll service.

## 6. Training plan (training)

| Weeks | Helper learns to |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Set up and protect the work area, carry and set ladders safely, run the vacuum, clean up |
| 3–4 | Sweep a flue with supervision, take the standard photo set, talk to customers at the door |
| 5–8 | Sweep on their own, help with caps and crown repairs, mix mortar |
| 9–12 | Assist on liners and rebuilds, spot common problems and explain them to Andrew |

Andrew's F.I.R.E. certification is the standard the crew learns to. Write down every "here's how we do it" moment in a shared note. That becomes the company manual.

## 9. Referral partners (business development)

Realtors and home inspectors see chimney problems every week. One good relationship can send a dozen jobs a year.

**Who to contact:** the 10 busiest listing agents in each service area, and every home inspector who doesn't do chimney work themselves.

**First message (text or email):**
> Hi {{name}}, I'm Andrew Paterini, owner of First Chimney and a F.I.R.E. Certified Technician. When a chimney comes up in one of your deals, I can usually get out quickly and give a plain answer with photos. Here's a page you can send your clients: firstchimneycontracting.com/realtors. My cell is (757) 589-0783.

**Keep it going:** answer their calls first, send a thank-you after every referral, and one useful email a month (W11 handles this).
